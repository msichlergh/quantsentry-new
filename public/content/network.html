How it works

Share the signal,
not the book.

A member submits a finding as a pseudonymised fingerprint. Another member asks whether an applicant matches anything. The answer is yes or no, never whose book it came from.
01

Submit

A confirmed finding is reduced to a set of hashed signals: device, payment method, session and behaviour. No names, no trades, no account numbers.

02

Query

When an applicant arrives, a member asks the pool whether anything matches. The answer comes back in a moment, with a confidence, not a dossier.

03

Decide

A match is a reason to look harder, not an instruction. Members investigate and decide under their own Terms of Service, as always.

The rules of the pool

What is shared,
and what never is.

Shared
  • Hashed device, payment and session signals
  • The type of finding, and when it was confirmed
  • Whether a signal is still matching or has expired
Never shared
  • Trades, balances or anything from a member's book
  • Which firm submitted a finding
  • Trader names, emails or account numbers
  • Anything at all if you have not opted in

Why members join

The operator counts on
you working alone.

Screen at application

A signal on an applicant arrives before they trade, rather than after a payout has been requested.

Findings age out

Signals expire when they stop matching, so nobody carries a permanent mark from a single finding.

Reciprocity, not surveillance

You get out what the pool puts in. Members submit findings and query them, and the pool never becomes a rating of a person.

Founding members shape the rules.

The network is in design. What gets shared, how long it lives and what a match is allowed to mean are being written now, with the firms who join first.
